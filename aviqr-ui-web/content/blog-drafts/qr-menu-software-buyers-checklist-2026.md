# What to Look for in QR Menu Software: A 2026 Buyer's Checklist

Every QR menu platform's homepage looks roughly the same: a phone mockup, a QR code, a "scan to order" line. The actual differences only show up once you're comparing pricing pages and feature lists side by side — and by then it's easy to miss something that matters until you've already committed.

Here's what's actually worth checking, in the order it tends to matter.

## 1. How the pricing actually works

There are two common models: a flat monthly subscription, or a percentage commission on every order (sometimes both at once). Run your own numbers — a commission-based platform that looks free upfront can end up costing far more than a flat subscription once you're doing real volume. At ₹999/month for AviQR's Growth plan, for example, that's a fixed cost regardless of whether you do 50 orders or 500 that month — worth comparing against what X% per order would actually total for you.

Also check: is there a genuinely free tier, or just a time-limited trial? A free tier with real (if limited) capacity — AviQR's Starter plan supports up to 20 menu items and 50 orders/day at zero cost — lets you actually run it for a while before deciding, rather than being pushed into a decision within two weeks.

## 2. How long setup actually takes

Ask specifically: can you go from signup to a live, working QR code the same day, without a sales call? Platforms that require onboarding calls or an "implementation team" are telling you something about ongoing friction, not just setup friction.

The single biggest time-saver here is OCR menu upload — photographing your existing printed menu and having it auto-build the digital version, instead of manually typing every item, price, and category. That alone is the difference between a 5-minute setup and an hour of data entry.

## 3. Whether it's just a menu, or an actual kitchen system

A QR menu that only takes orders and dumps them somewhere doesn't remove your kitchen's bottleneck — it just relocates it. Check specifically for:

- **Kitchen Display (KOT)** — does the order route directly to a kitchen screen, or does someone still have to relay it verbally or on paper?
- **POS/billing integration** — does a QR order become a real, GST-compliant invoice automatically, or is billing a separate manual step?
- **Inventory tracking** — can it tell you real ingredient cost per dish and warn you before you run out of something mid-service?

## 4. Language support, if your customers need it

If a meaningful share of your customers are more comfortable reading a menu in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Bengali, Marathi, or another regional language, check whether translation is automatic and customer-selectable on the menu itself — or whether you'd need to build and maintain separate menus per language yourself. AviQR handles this with 9 Indian languages selectable directly on the customer-facing menu, no per-language setup required on your end.

## 5. Payment options

UPI is table stakes in India at this point — if a platform doesn't support it natively, that's disqualifying on its own. Beyond that, check whether card and cash are both still available as fallback options, since not every customer wants to pay online, and forcing it can lose the sale entirely.

## 6. What happens as you grow

A few questions worth asking before you're locked in:

- Multi-outlet support — if you open a second location, does it need a separate account, or does one login manage both?
- Does it handle more than one venue type, if that's relevant to you (a hotel needing room service, a food court needing multi-vendor billing)?
- Loyalty/CRM — since a QR-ordering customer is already identified by phone number, does the platform let you build repeat-visit loyalty on top of that data, or is it a bolt-on you'd need a separate tool for?

## The short version

If you only check three things: **the real cost at your actual order volume (not the headline price)**, **whether it's an actual kitchen/billing system or just a pretty menu**, and **how long it genuinely takes to get live**. Everything else is easier to fix later than a bad decision on those three.
